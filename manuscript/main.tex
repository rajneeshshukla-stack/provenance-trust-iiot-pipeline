\abstract{Authenticated industrial telemetry can contain conflicting versions of the same observation. We investigate how per-identity versus feed-level conflict containment affects correctness, availability and forged-data exposure in brokered edge-to-cloud pipelines. Provenance-Bound Trust-Aware Ingestion (PBTI) integrates signed origin and relay evidence, policy-controlled admission, per-identity conflict handling and auditable recovery; a controlled feed-level comparator shares the other mechanisms. In single-host simulations, the comparator matched PBTI's final forged admissions in all \VPOneIdentical\ main scenarios and final losses in \VOTwoBFiveIdentical; the remaining difference reflected reviewer-enabled recovery. With one-shot 60~s review, feed-level containment delayed genuine data by an additional \WAoneEfiveBFivehistvsPBTIWdshort~s per observation under stolen keys while reducing forged-data exposure in a mixed workload; with matched event-driven reviewers, the primary outcomes were identical or equivalent within predefined margins in four workloads. Under equivocation with suppressed messages, feed-level holds retained fewer forgeries; selective holds removed them in amendment-informed cells at a cost in genuine data but failed their registered grid criteria. No evaluated rule contained a consistent impersonator holding the relevant keys. PBTI achieved \RTpRatioBTwo\ times the in-process throughput and \RWireRatioBTwo\ times the message size of a signature-free Zero-Trust baseline. The findings characterize conditional security--availability trade-offs, not general superiority or deployment validation.}

\keyword{Industrial Internet of Things; Zero Trust; data provenance; trustworthy data ingestion; edge-to-cloud; conflict containment; equivocation; replay protection; data lineage; MQTT}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Industrial organizations move process data from operational technology (OT) through edge gateways and message brokers into enterprise data platforms~\cite{iic_iira_v110}. Zero Trust removes implicit trust based on network location~\cite{nist_sp800_207}, and industrial Zero-Trust work concentrates on remote access, segmentation, lightweight authentication and broker-side access control~\cite{federici2023zt,zanasi2024flexzt,zhang2026lztaka,james2025mqttzt}: these mechanisms decide \emph{who may connect and what they may access}.

A data platform must also decide \emph{which data object becomes authoritative, why, and whether that decision can be re-checked later}. In brokered \pubsub\ deployments session identity ends at the broker: MQTT delivery guarantees hold per hop~\cite{oasis_mqtt5}, so a subscriber learns neither which device produced a message nor which edge workload relayed it unless that evidence travels with the message, and OPC UA PubSub's replay protection depends on optional configuration~\cite{opcua_part2}. Retransmission after reconnects, late backlogs and clock faults complicate the decision: a legitimate retransmission and a malicious replay are byte-identical, and a firmware fault and an attacker holding a stolen key can both produce two authenticated observations with the same identity.

Once two authenticated versions of one event exist, the pipeline must contain the conflict: it can withhold only the affected event identities and keep the rest of the device's feed available, or withhold the feed from the fork point until a reviewer resolves it, as signed-log systems do when they ignore or expose a forked feed~\cite{tarr2019ssb,haeberlen2007peerreview}. The first choice limits availability loss but leaves a forged version authoritative whenever no contradiction arrives; the second withholds forged and genuine data alike for as long as review takes. We found no comparison of the two granularities for IIoT telemetry under matched admission, credentials and review (Section~\ref{sec:related}); this describes our search, not a priority claim.

This study makes that comparison with \emph{Provenance-Bound Trust-Aware Ingestion} (\sys), an ingestion architecture that carries origin, relay, authorization and history evidence with each observation across a broker, and a feed-level comparator, B5, that shares every \sys\ mechanism except the conflict response. We ask:
\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{RQ1} When cryptographically authenticated IIoT telemetry contains conflicting event versions, what are the correctness, availability, contamination-duration and recovery consequences of per-identity versus feed-level conflict containment under matched admission and review assumptions?
\item[] \textbf{RQ2} Under which attack and delivery-fault conditions is either granularity, or a selective-hold combination of the two, preferable, and under which conditions do all of them fail?
\item[] \textbf{RQ3} What processing, message-size, memory and storage costs does object-level provenance binding add on the evaluated host, and which deployment properties remain unmeasured?
\end{itemize}

The contributions differ in kind: (C1, \emph{architectural integration and implementation}) an ingestion architecture for observations crossing an untrusted broker that combines an origin statement, a relay countersignature naming an authorized edge workload, versioned path-bound admission, credential lifecycle with retroactive taint, signed review and Merkle-checkpointed evidence, with a reference implementation (Section~\ref{sec:arch}); (C2, \emph{elementary formal refinement}) Rule L, a per-identity conflict rule with a locality lemma that restates the scope of its update rule: without intervening lifecycle transitions, $k$ admitted statements change the class and authority of at most $2k$ event identities (Proposition~\ref{prop:locality}); (C3, \emph{experimental characterization}) a comparison with a feed-level comparator, registered in advance in the repository (self-recorded), that receives the same keys, policy, evidence and, in matched arms, the same reviewer and schedule, so that it can only withhold more and expose less and the experiments measure by how much and when (Sections~\ref{sec:methods} and~\ref{sec:results}); (C4, \emph{empirical finding}) a time-dependent characterization of the trade-off (exposure, withheld time, deadline availability, recovery); and (C5, \emph{empirical finding}) the failure conditions: full impersonation with both keys, suppressed contradictions, the costs of selective holds and reviewed release under compromised credentials. Every building block has a published analogue (Table~\ref{tab:related}); we claim no new cryptographic primitive, no superiority of either strategy, no deployment validation and no evidence that an authenticated measurement is physically correct.

\section{Related Work and Research Gap}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Access-level Zero Trust.} NIST SP 800-207 places per-request access decisions at decision and enforcement points~\cite{nist_sp800_207}. Industrial work applies it to remote maintenance~\cite{federici2023zt}, decentralized overlays with certificate-based identity and revocation~\cite{zanasi2024flexzt}, lightweight key agreement~\cite{zhang2026lztaka}, broker-side controls~\cite{james2025mqttzt} and device credentials split into technical and operational credentials~\cite{lazzaro2026vciot}; all decide at the point of access, and data-pillar guidance labels and tags data objects without addressing their provenance~\cite{nsa2024datapillar}.

\textbf{End-to-end protection through brokers.} ENTRUST (per-topic keys and per-publisher counters that detect duplicated or dropped messages)~\cite{dahlmanns2021entrust}, MQTT-I (sequence numbers and signed, chained Merkle roots)~\cite{buccafurri2024mqtti}, SMQTT (key-server-managed authenticity and replay protection)~\cite{yousaf2026smqtt} and OSCORE-IoT~\cite{dighriri2026oscoreiot} protect \pubsub\ end to end, AUPS is an authenticated \pubsub\ system for the IoT~\cite{rizzardi2016aups}, and blockchain anchoring adds accountability~\cite{lazzaro2024mqtt}. In the IETF ACE \pubsub\ profile for CoAP, an Internet-Draft, publishers countersign their group-encrypted objects and subscribers keep a per-publisher replay window that discards a second object with an already seen sequence number; the draft describes no reporting of such conflicts~\cite{ietf_ace_coap_pubsub}. The relay statement of \sys\ binds the material of an RFC~9338 countersignature~\cite{rfc9338} and adds the workload identity and path authorization that RFC~9338 leaves to the application. Sparkplug numbers messages modulo 256 and covers security only in non-normative guidance~\cite{sparkplug3}.

\textbf{Signed logs, transparency and provenance.} Signed provenance chains~\cite{hasan2009tos}, tamper-evident logs~\cite{crosby2009tamper} and Certificate Transparency~\cite{rfc9162} provide append-only, tamper-evident evidence; the IETF SCITT architecture registers signed statements under a registration policy, with receipts and registration checks that auditors can reproduce~\cite{rfc9943}, but defines no per-event delivery-history semantics and leaves revocation strategies for compromised keys out of scope. IoT provenance schemes use physical unclonable functions or wireless fingerprints~\cite{aman2017provenance,aman2019provenance}, blockchains~\cite{javaid2018blockpro,sigwart2019bcprov}, device-signed hash-linked readouts~\cite{saito2025authenticity} or signed records with a key-compromise history~\cite{tsai2026actuation}; Sigwart et al. leave the correctness of records at entry, the admission question addressed here, to future work~\cite{sigwart2019bcprov}. C2PA judges signer revocation against a trusted time-stamp~\cite{c2pa25}; our compromise time corresponds to the X.509 invalidity date~\cite{rfc5280}. Lineage is traced in stream processing~\cite{palyvos2019genealog}, for machine-learning artefacts~\cite{sukumaran2026provenance} and in W3C PROV~\cite{w3c_prov_dm}, and intrusion-recovery systems repair dependent state from externally identified attacks or retroactive patches~\cite{goel2005taser,chandra2011warp}.

\textbf{Responses to forks.} SUNDR's fork consistency makes a server's concealment of updates detectable once clients compare views~\cite{li2004sundr}; Secure Scuttlebutt, whose feeds share the structure of our observation statement (author key, sequence, previous hash, Ed25519 over canonical JSON~\cite{ssb_protocol_guide}), ignores a feed from the fork onward~\cite{tarr2019ssb}; PeerReview turns equivocation into verifiable evidence that auditors replay~\cite{haeberlen2007peerreview}; the Global Data Plane names holes and branches in signed edge-data streams and accepts them as a partial order~\cite{mor2019gdp}; Byzantine-fault-tolerant CRDTs accept equivocating updates as concurrent operations and still converge~\cite{kleppmann2022bftcrdt}; and the 2P-BFT-Log preprint lets correct replicas agree on the earliest fork and a proof of it, after which the forked log cannot be extended~\cite{lavoie2023twopbftlog}. They address detection and convergence, not the availability of individual events of a feed.

\textbf{Edge-to-cloud data engineering.} WedgeChain lets a cloud node certify digests of client-signed entries committed at an untrusted edge~\cite{nawab2021wedgechain}. Kafka's idempotent producer de-duplicates retries by producer identifier, epoch and sequence~\cite{kip98}, and CloudEvents consumers may treat events with the same source and identifier as duplicates~\cite{cloudevents102}; both make a conflicting second version indistinguishable from a retry. IIoT pipeline studies evaluate store-and-forward recovery without security~\cite{matias2026resilient}, a counter reset after a reboot enables replay in LoRaWAN~\cite{yang2018lorawan} (hence the boot epoch), and trust scores rate data~\cite{lim2010trust} or data sources~\cite{gigli2025zonia} rather than bind admission to identity.

\textbf{Research gap.} Table~\ref{tab:related} separates what prior work provides from what this study integrates, changes and evaluates. SCITT already provides policy-gated registration with reproducible checks, so \sys\ is a telemetry-specific integration, not a new transparency primitive. What we did not find in the works examined (Table~\Sref{tab:S-coverage} and those above) is an evaluation of the \emph{conflict-response granularity} itself for IIoT telemetry, with a feed-level comparator that shares all other mechanisms, matched review schedules and time-dependent outcomes; that evaluation is the main addition.

\section{System and Threat Model}\label{sec:model}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the data path. Signing-capable devices sign each observation; for legacy devices the polling edge workload is the origin signer (gateway attestation). Each edge workload has a short-lived credential and a durable store-and-forward buffer, and messages cross an MQTT broker that keeps mutual TLS~\cite{rfc9846} and access-control lists as defence in depth but is not trusted for data-object integrity. The cloud-side verifier is a policy enforcement point for data objects: it consults an identity authority (certificate validation, rotation, revocation with effective time $t_\mathrm{rev}$ and optional compromise time $t_\mathrm{comp}$) and a versioned policy store, and writes an event registry, a content-addressed raw store, a quarantine archive and an evidence log whose signed checkpoints are copied to a witness. Downstream processing derives event-time windows and a current-value table with versioned lineage; an auditor re-verifies the evidence offline. The verifier clock is trusted for horizons and taint; device time stamps are event time, never proof of time.

Protected assets are observations with their identities, timestamps, sequence information and provenance links, committed raw data, policy history, credentials, lineage, derived data and audit evidence. The trusted computing base comprises the cryptographic primitives, the identity authority's revocation path, the verifier at decision time (including its clock and evidence key, except in insider class T5), policy-history integrity and at least one witness. The device-to-gateway link is assumed not to be attacker-writable (an authenticated fieldbus or device session); Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what happens otherwise. Table~\ref{tab:threat} lists attacker positions and modelled failures, several of which are indistinguishable by their observable effects. Excluded are breaks of Ed25519 or SHA-256, compromise of the identity authority with all witnesses, process or sensor manipulation before signing, safety and actuation paths, supply-chain and side-channel attacks, confidentiality and flooding attacks.

\section{PBTI Architecture and Formal Properties}\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Statements and Admission}\label{sec:mech}

A \sys\ message is a JSON object in the RFC~8785 JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS)~\cite{rfc8785}, $\{\Sobs, \sigma_o, \Srel, \sigma_w\}$ (fields in Table~\Sref{tab:S-fields}). The origin signer signs the observation statement with Ed25519~\cite{rfc8032}, $\sigma_o=\mathrm{Sign}(k_o, D_\mathrm{obs}\,\|\,\mathrm{JCS}(\Sobs))$, and the edge workload signs the relay statement, $\sigma_w=\mathrm{Sign}(k_w, D_\mathrm{rel}\,\|\,\mathrm{JCS}(\Srel))$, with domain-separation prefixes $D$. The relay statement contains $\mathit{odig}=H(\mathrm{JCS}(\Sobs)\,\|\,\sigma_o)$ and therefore binds exactly one signed observation. The event identity $e=(\mathit{did},\mathit{ep},\mathit{seq})$ is assigned by the producer and signed; the content digest is $\mathit{cd}=H(\mathrm{JCS}(\Sobs))$ with $H$ = SHA-256, and each observation names its predecessor's digest in \textsf{prev}. Canonicalization and deterministic signatures make benign re-encodings and retransmissions byte-identical; for legacy devices the edge workload assigns $(\mathit{ep},\mathit{seq},\mathit{prev})$ in an epoch namespace unique to that workload.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:admit} lists the admission checks in order; the first failing check determines one of five dispositions (ACCEPT, DUPLICATE, CONFLICT, QUARANTINE, REJECT), and every receipt produces exactly one evidence record. The policy decision point evaluates a versioned, schema-checked document that assigns devices to assets and schemas, states whether a device must sign its observations or may be gateway-attested, and lists the devices each workload may relay; the decision is a pure function of the policy version in force at verifier time $t$ and the statements' identities, so an auditor can re-check it.

\subsection{Rule L and Its Locality}\label{sec:rulel}

For each identity $e$, let $V(e)$ be the set of admitted content digests and $I(e)$ the digests asserted for $e$ by admitted successors' \textsf{prev} links. A slot with an admitted version is COMMITTED when $V(e)\cup I(e)$ contains exactly one distinct digest and CONTESTED when it contains at least two. Without an admitted version it is ABSENT, even if an expected digest is known, or LOST if its gap was closed after the gap horizon; an authorized release can later admit a version into a LOST slot (Table~\Sref{tab:S-transitions}; slot transitions in Figure~\Sref{fig:S-state}). Because versions and implied digests accumulate as sets, the classification is independent of delivery order for a fixed admitted evidence set, a contested identity never becomes committed again, and a gap-fill forgery is contested once a contradictory genuine version or successor link is admitted. Identical contents are idempotent, so the RETRY/SUSPECTED\_REPLAY annotation (same relay, within 30~s) has no authority. Freshness, revocation, policy changes, sequence limits and gap closure can change \emph{which} evidence is admitted, so the pipeline as a whole is not claimed to be invariant under arbitrary arrival schedules.

\begin{Proposition}[Locality of Rule L]\label{prop:locality}
Let $(V,I)$ be an evidence state, $\tau$ the taint flags and $\lambda$ the gap-closure flags, and let $S$ be a sequence of $k$ statements admitted by \sys\ in its main configuration, with no lifecycle transition (gap closure, revocation processing, or a reviewer decision other than the admission it re-runs) in between. Let $A(S)$ be the set of the identities of the statements in $S$ and, for each statement with a positive sequence number and a non-null \textsf{prev}, its predecessor identity. Then $V$ and $I$ change only on $A(S)$; $|A(S)|\le 2k$, with equality exactly when every statement contributes a predecessor and these $2k$ identities are pairwise distinct; and the Rule L class (including LOST) and the authority of every identity outside $A(S)$ are unchanged.
\end{Proposition}
\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} Admitting $s$ adds $\mathit{cd}(s)$ to $V$ only at $e(s)$ and, if its sequence number is positive and $\mathsf{prev}(s)\neq\bot$, adds $\mathsf{prev}(s)$ to $I$ only at the predecessor of $e(s)$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:admit}); by induction over $S$, $V$ and $I$ are unchanged outside $A(S)$. The class of an identity $x$ is a function of $V(x)$, $I(x)$ and $\lambda(x)$, and its authority in addition of $\tau(x)$; $\lambda$ is written only by gap closure and $\tau$ only by revocation processing, both excluded. $A(S)$ is the union of at most $k$ statement identities and at most $k$ predecessors. $\square$

The argument is elementary; it was cross-checked by an implementation-independent model and by bounded enumeration on the verifier (Section~\ref{sec:formal}). The bound is attained, for example, by $k$ equivocations at non-adjacent sequence numbers whose \textsf{prev} contradicts a committed predecessor; because admission first advances time-driven lifecycle processing, the hypothesis is a condition on the schedule. B5 (Section~\ref{sec:pipelines}), by contrast, removes every identity of the feed from the fork point onward until review. The proposition bounds the state changes caused by \emph{admitted} contradictory evidence; it does not establish that a contradiction is ever admitted (a suppressed one never is) and excludes lifecycle transitions, admission decisions that earlier admissions change, downstream fan-out, selective holds and replicated verifiers (Section~\Sref{sec:S-protocol}).

\subsection{Credential Lifecycle, Review and Release}\label{sec:lifecycle}

\textbf{Credential lifecycle and retroactive containment.} Expired but unrevoked keys are accepted within a rotation grace period intended for buffered data. After a revocation is observed, ordinary admission under the revoked origin or relay key is refused or, for buffered statements, quarantined (REVOKED\_BUFFERED). If the revocation supplies a compromise time $t_\mathrm{comp}$, committed slots whose \emph{origin} key is revoked and whose verifier commit time is at or after $t_\mathrm{comp}$ are TAINTED: raw bytes are kept, the observation leaves derived processing and affected windows are recomputed. The selector uses commit time, not proof of creation time, so it can exclude legitimate buffered data; a revised record can only move $t_\mathrm{comp}$ earlier. Device-signed observations are not tainted when only their relaying workload was compromised, since the relay could not alter them.

\textbf{Quarantine review and release.} Each quarantined receipt opens a case (QUARANTINED $\rightarrow$ REVIEW\_PENDING $\rightarrow$ RELEASED or REJECTED). A reviewer listed in the policy signs a domain-separated decision bound to the case, the release reason and a snapshot of the evidence examined; the verifier checks the reviewer's authorization and revocation status, the case state and snapshot freshness. A release re-runs admission with only the original quarantine reason waived, so every other check, including Rule L, still applies, and every decision is a signed evidence record that the auditor replays. A REVOKED\_BUFFERED waiver additionally requires a release-evidence condition: \emph{none} (no release under a revoked key), \emph{discretionary} (the observation's own time stamp precedes $t_\mathrm{comp}$; used in every scenario campaign) or \emph{witness} (a token from a time-stamping witness outside the verifier for the exact statement digest, dated before the earliest plausible compromise time). The discretionary condition is a policy condition, not proof of pre-compromise existence (Section~\ref{sec:res-release}). The automated reviewer of the experiments releases a case only if hash links corroborate it: the slot holds that single digest, and the predecessor is authoritative with the digest named by \textsf{prev}, or an admitted successor's \textsf{prev} names it. This is a consistency check under the signers' keys, not independent evidence.

\subsection{Evidence, Lineage and State Model}\label{sec:state}

Accepted messages are stored write-once under $H(m)$, other receipts in the quarantine archive. Evidence records are leaves of RFC~9162 Merkle trees~\cite{rfc9162} over batches of up to 512 records (at least every 5~s); each batch root is signed, chained and copied to the witness. Each derived version stores its sorted input references, their Merkle root and its cause (new, late, contest, taint or release); earlier versions are retained, and the lineage maps onto W3C PROV~\cite{w3c_prov_dm}. The state model uses four orthogonal machines (receipt disposition, event-identity slot, credential lifecycle, versioned derived records) plus a review case per quarantined receipt (Figure~\Sref{fig:S-state}, Table~\Sref{tab:S-transitions}); ten invariants and their oracles are in Table~\Sref{tab:S-invariants}.

\subsection{Verification of the Formal Properties}\label{sec:formal}

The signature bindings rely on the assumed security of Ed25519, SHA-256 and domain separation; there is no computational proof of the complete system. The symbolic model was \VFormalSymbolic\ (ProVerif~\cite{blanchet2016proverif}); these correspondences hold under the model's assumptions. That the implementation realizes the update rule of Proposition~\ref{prop:locality} rests on automated code inspection and bounded checks of an implementation-independent model (\WAfourMcases\ cases), the unmodified verifier with real signatures (\WAfourLexhaustive\ exhaustive and \WAfourLrandom\ sampled batches) and all combinations of up to four lifecycle operations (\WAfourCexhaustive\ exhaustive, \WAfourCrandom\ sampled longer sequences) under nine invariants (\WAfourAllPass; Table~\Sref{tab:S-bounded}). They found one defect, fixed before the confirmatory runs: the taint selector ignored a revised revocation record with an earlier compromise time (no scenario revokes a key twice); an earlier bounded checker found an unrecorded exception for numbers outside the I-JSON range, which all pipelines now reject with an evidence record. Bounded results do not extend to unbounded feeds, arbitrary schedules or replicated verifiers.

\section{Experimental Methodology}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Testbed and Scenarios}\label{sec:testbed}

The security and correctness experiments use a deterministic discrete-event testbed with four cells of one gateway and six devices (one legacy device per gateway), 1~Hz per device for 300~s (7200 observations per run) and a 180~s drain. Gateways implement durable store-and-forward (2~s retransmission timeout, 60 messages/s backlog drain, unbounded buffers with a backlog limit in messages, not bytes, acknowledgement once the verifier has ingested an entry), and the network adds 20~ms plus exponential jitter and injects faults per scenario (Table~\ref{tab:scen}). Protocol time is virtual; processing cost is measured in wall-clock time. Separate generators for production, network, attacker decisions and rendering, all derived from one seed, give every pipeline identical schedules (hence paired comparisons); attacks are rendered into each pipeline's wire format using only the keys the attacker holds. A sixteenth, \emph{mixed} workload combines all attack and failure classes with a 120~s horizon, a device move with a policy update and a revocation with a compromise window.

\subsection{Pipelines and Review Schedules}\label{sec:pipelines}

All pipelines share storage, downstream processing and the policy document and differ in admission and conflict-response semantics (repository \texttt{docs/BASELINES.md}). \textbf{B1 (conventional)} authenticates the gateway TLS session once, permits plant-wide publication, de-duplicates message identifiers within 300~s and upserts by (device, timestamp). \textbf{B2 (Zero Trust)} verifies the workload credential, the path-bound policy of \sys\ and freshness per message, without object signatures. \textbf{B3 (provenance)} verifies origin signatures without rotation grace and keeps a hash-chained ledger, approximating signature and hash-chain provenance schemes~\cite{hasan2009tos,javaid2018blockpro,sigwart2019bcprov}. \textbf{B4 (mainstream combination)} adds to B2 origin signatures on the observation statement, rotation grace, first-writer-wins admission on the producer-assigned event identity, as CloudEvents keys on it~\cite{cloudevents102}, and B3's ledger, but no relay statement, predecessor-link contest, quarantine workflow or taint.

\textbf{B5 (feed-level comparator)} keeps every mechanism of \sys\ except the conflict response: a receipt that adds new fork evidence (a second admitted content for its identity, an admitted successor link naming another digest for it, or a predecessor link naming none of the admitted versions of its predecessor, as in SSB and PeerReview~\cite{tarr2019ssb,haeberlen2007peerreview}) holds the affected (device, epoch) feed from the fork point onward; every held observation is a review case, and an automated reviewer releases held and releasable quarantined observations that satisfy the hash-link corroboration rule of Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle}. All B5 and B5s results of the simulation stages come from the corrected implementation of this specification (Section~\ref{sec:impl}); the historical results are kept in Document S1 and Data S1. A released held observation does not re-run admission, which it had passed on arrival, and a feed resumes only when none of its hold cases is undecided; because every new arrival on a forked feed opens a case younger than $R$, under a slow schedule a forked feed does not resume before production ends (in E5 with \Bhist, seeds 21--23: forks at 30--50~s, resumption at 360--370~s, after production ended at 300~s), so B5's withholding reflects both the schedule and this resume rule. Within a periodic pass, cases are reviewed in identity order, which lets a released predecessor corroborate its successor in the same pass, for B5 and PBTI-R alike; contested identities get no case. Because B5 adds a hold condition to the authority predicate of \sys\ and releases a held observation only when Rule L commits it and a hash link corroborates it, its authoritative set is never larger than that of PBTI-R with the same reviewer and schedule: it can retain fewer, but never more, forgeries and withhold more, but never less, genuine data (no outcome violated it in any of the \XSubsetRuns\ matched runs), so the matched comparison measures the size of these differences, not their sign.

The reviewer is our design and its schedule a parameter of the comparison. The \emph{one-shot} rule rejects an uncorroborated case at its first review and runs with minimum case age $R=60$~s and review cadence $C=10$~s (\Bhist); the \emph{deferring} rule keeps an uncorroborated, uncontradicted case pending and runs with $R\in\{0,1,5,60\}$~s and $C\in\{1,10\}$~s; and \emph{event-driven} review (\Bev) re-examines, right after an admission, the cases whose evidence it can have changed ($R=0$ with a periodic scheduler is not event-driven). \textbf{B5s} disables recovery and keeps the feed hold (the SSB rule). \textbf{PBTI-R} applies the same reviewer and schedules to the quarantine cases of \sys\ (\Rhist, \Rev), so PBTI-R versus B5 under the same schedule is the matched-review comparison, whereas \sys\ versus B5 also differs in active recovery. \textbf{PBTI-SH} adds selective evidence-gated holds to \sys: from its first contested identity a feed is suspect for the rest of the epoch, and a committed observation of a suspect feed that no admitted successor link corroborates is withheld, retroactively, until such a link arrives or, in the variant PBTI-SH-T30, until a 30~s timeout; contested or tainted observations are never released, and no evidence source is added.

\subsection{Outcomes}\label{sec:metrics}

At the end of each run the oracle counts non-legitimate authoritative observations (O1: content matching no legitimate observation) and legitimate observations produced by honest devices and accepted by a gateway but absent from the authoritative set (O2), and separately held or quarantined observations, duplicates and corrupt derived windows; release latency runs from production to authority for observations eventually released. Cost metrics are service time, throughput, CPU, edge signing cost, message and storage bytes, queue depth and end-to-end latency. Because final counts measure neither how long forged data were authoritative nor how long genuine data were withheld, a transition tracer outside the pipeline records, in virtual time, every change of the authoritative set $A(t)$ of (event identity, content key) pairs, where the content key is the oracle's content identity (device, asset, timestamp, schema, canonical payload); a forged content under a genuine identity is a different pair and never counts as availability of the genuine observation. Over the fixed interval from the start of production to the end of the drain, each run yields the non-legitimate exposure NLX (record-seconds during which non-legitimate pairs are authoritative), the withheld time $W$ (mean seconds per gateway-accepted legitimate observation during which it is not authoritative up to the end of the drain, so that observations still missing then contribute as end-of-run loss) and AV5 (the share of legitimate observations first available within 5~s); secondary outcomes (other deadlines, Kaplan--Meier quantiles with censoring, withdrawals, freshness, containment latency, derived-window exposure, alerts of an illustrative monitoring workload) are defined in Table~\Sref{tab:S-tm-def}. Because $W$ and NLX integrate to the end of the fixed 180~s drain, an observation that is still missing then and a forgery retained to the end contribute in proportion to the drain; an exploratory screen with shorter drains on the historical rows, not a rerun with hypothesis tests, changed the margin class of 25 of the 222 registered $W$ comparisons, all in the selective-hold analyses, and no gate outcome (Section~\Sref{sec:S-env}). Fifteen hand-computed traces test the tracer, every run checks its final authoritative set against the pipeline's, and the run is the unit of analysis.

\subsection{Evaluation Stages, Registration and Evidence Levels}\label{sec:design}

Results carry one of five evidence levels (stages, designs, run counts and levels in Table~\Sref{tab:S-levels}): A, confirmatory and registered in advance (predictions P1--P3 of the main evaluation, the review-schedule evaluation and the selective-hold grid); B, exploratory or descriptive (the comparisons with B1--B4, P4, the mixed workload, the re-execution with the tracer, the selective-hold controls, the sensitivity analyses, the SKAB replay and the attacker-model campaign); C, amendment-informed (the selective-hold factorial cells); D, formal or deterministic verification (proof, symbolic model, bounded checks, lifecycle analysis and control traces); and E, performance on one host. The first of two analysis plans registers P1--P4 (P1: B5 equals \sys\ on O1 in all 15 scenarios; P2: B5s exceeds \sys\ on O2 by the practical threshold in E5 and E8; P3: B5's O2 is within that threshold of \sys\ in every scenario, but held observations become authoritative only after $\ge R$; P4: B4 remains worse than \sys\ on O1 in E5 and E14) and decision rules for comparative wording; the second registers hypotheses H1a--H1d and H3a--H3c, outcomes, comparison families, practical margins and claim gates for the review-schedule and selective-hold evaluations on fresh seeds 21--40. Both are self-recorded in the repository with SHA-256 hashes, not deposited externally (a hash shows that a file is unchanged, not when it was written), and both were written by the automated process after it had seen earlier results: the first after the B1--B4 comparisons (so P4 is descriptive) and after smoke runs on seed 1 used to debug B5, the second after the main evaluation and single-seed development runs that already showed event-driven B5 close to \sys\ in E5. Fresh seeds therefore guard against seed-specific artefacts, not against design knowledge. One amendment, recorded after a one-seed pilot and before any confirmatory selective-hold run, added the eight factorial cells with equivocation high, chosen with knowledge of the sensitivity analysis; the selective-hold rule had been designed on four of them with seeds 1--5, and results depending on the amendment are labelled amendment-informed. Two later additions keep their own labels: the rerun of every B5 and B5s specification of the simulation stages with the corrected comparator applies the registered outcomes, families, margins and gates unchanged but is a deviation decided after the results were known (DV8), and the attacker-model campaign is exploratory (DV9). Timestamps, hashes, amendments and deviations are in Table~\Sref{tab:S-chronology}; each principal finding is mapped to its evidence level in Table~\Sref{tab:S-findings}.


\subsection{Statistical Analysis}\label{sec:stats}

We report means with 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples; the generator of each comparison is seeded from its key, a derivation the analysis plan does not state, Table~\Sref{tab:S-chronology}). Paired outcomes are compared with two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (zero differences discarded, SciPy default), and pairs identical in every seed are reported as identical and enter Holm correction~\cite{holm1979} with $p=1$. In the main evaluation the registered family is 15 scenarios $\times$ 2 comparisons (\sys\ versus B5 and B5s) per outcome, with practical thresholds of one observation per run (O1) and 1\% of the legitimate observations, 72 (O2); the comparisons with B1--B4 and the sensitivity, replay and mixed-workload tests are exploratory and Holm-corrected within their families, and wall-clock metrics use Mann--Whitney tests with Holm correction and Cliff's $\delta$. In the review-schedule and selective-hold evaluations every comparison is classified mechanically from its paired differences, with practical margins fixed in the registration: one observation per run (O1), 1\% of the cell's reachable observations (O2), 60 record-seconds (NLX), 0.6~s (W, below the 1~s sampling period) and 0.01 (AV5). A comparison is \emph{identical} if equal in every seed; \emph{equivalent within the margin} if the 90\% bootstrap interval of the mean difference lies inside $\pm$margin (the interval form of two one-sided tests at the 5\% level~\cite{schuirmann1987}); \emph{better} or \emph{worse beyond the margin} if the Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon test is significant and the 95\% interval lies beyond the margin; otherwise \emph{significant, margin unresolved} (registered label ``different within the margin''; the Holm-adjusted test is significant but the 95\% interval crosses a margin boundary, which establishes neither equivalence nor a difference beyond the margin, whichever side the point estimate lies on) or \emph{inconclusive}. Equivalence takes precedence and is never inferred from non-significance; a family is one campaign and one outcome.

\subsection{Implementation, Reproducibility and Verification}\label{sec:impl}

The reference implementation (Python~3.13; Ed25519, SHA-256 and X.509~\cite{rfc5280} through the \texttt{cryptography} package) uses device certificates with a \texttt{urn:pbti:device} URI and workload certificates with a SPIFFE URI~\cite{spiffe_spec}. A Rego formulation for Open Policy Agent~\cite{opa}, run with the rego-cpp interpreter (regopy 1.5.2; OPA itself was not run), matched the Python decision point on all \VParityWellFormedN\ well-formed and \VParityMalformedReqN\ malformed requests of a parity harness. Storage is in memory for virtual-time runs and SQLite with write-ahead logging and group commit for wall-clock runs. The tracer, the review rules, PBTI-SH and the optional witness are separate code paths, inactive unless enabled.

\textbf{Re-execution and automated checks.} Re-executing the main evaluation and mixed workload reproduced all \WAzeroIdentical\ of \WAzeroCompared\ stored runs field by field, and re-executing selected seeds of every main and confirmatory campaign in a separate cloud environment gave \XRxIdentical\ of \XRxRuns\ identical runs (Table~\Sref{tab:S-reexec}). Separate instances of the same AI model audited every pipeline's source code against the specification, re-implemented B5 from its specification as a reference model, recomputed the outcome definitions and registered statistics, and audited the locality proof (Section~\Sref{sec:S-env}); they found no defect in a primary outcome or registered classification of \sys, but two defects of B5 that can disadvantage it, two counter defects and the drain dependence of $W$ and NLX. These checks show determinism, portability and internal consistency, not validity, and none is an independent reproduction.

\textbf{Comparator correction.} The historical implementation of B5, which produced every frozen result, held released observations again when a late successor of a contested identity arrived without new fork evidence, could leave an observation held without an open review case, and counted some holds twice. We corrected these defects in a separate module and kept the historical module unchanged (its hash is in the freeze file); the six choices that the specification had left open are now stated in it. \YRegTests\ regression tests reproduce each defect on the historical code and check the corrected behaviour, the kept choices and the case without fork evidence, and the corrected implementation matched the reference model with the corrected choices in all \YRefN\ synthetic comparisons (final authoritative set and every authority interval). Every B5 and B5s specification of the simulation stages was rerun on its original seeds (\YRunsRerun\ runs; the performance runs of B5 contain no fork evidence and were not repeated) and analysed with the unchanged scripts, outcome definitions, comparison families, margins and gates. The correction changed no primary outcome of the main evaluation, the mixed workload, the re-execution with the tracer, the sweep or the replay, and none of B5s. It changed \YRunsChanged\ runs in which B5's reviewer acts quickly or factorial stressors combine, always in the same direction for the cell means: $W$ fell by up to \YCellAllWMaxDrop~s per observation, end-of-run losses by up to \YCellAllOTwoMaxDrop\ observations and AV5 rose by up to \YCellAllAVFiveMaxRise, whereas exposure rose by up to \YCellAllNLXMaxRise\ record-seconds, and in \YRunsOOneChanged\ runs one more forgery was retained. Of the \YCmpN\ registered comparisons, \YCmpEstChanged\ changed their estimate and \YCmpClassChanged\ their class; no claim gate or registered prediction changed. Decided after the results were known, the correction is a recorded deviation (DV8): the principal tables and figures use the corrected comparator, and Document S1 and Data S1 give the historical values and every difference (Table~\Sref{tab:S-v9-runs}).

\textbf{Use of generative AI.} The authors conceived the study and formulated the research problem, set the requirements and reporting rules that the work followed, and directed it. Under their direction, generative AI (Claude, Anthropic) designed and wrote the text, the code (prototype, baselines, testbed, extensions, analysis, the corrected comparator and its regression tests), the tests and the analysis plans; ran the tests, experiments, reruns and bounded checks in a cloud workspace; searched the literature and checked references against publisher and repository records; and carried out the re-executions and checks described above. \subsection{Main Evaluation across Scenarios}\label{sec:res-main}

Table~\Sref{tab:S-full} gives O1 and O2 of all pipelines in every scenario (intervals in Data~S1; the exploratory comparisons with B1--B4 are summarized in Section~\Sref{sec:S-main}). With a stolen device key alone (E5), the attacker could not produce a valid relay statement, so \sys\ and B5 rejected every broker injection, whereas B1--B3 admitted them and B4 admitted \RAttrEFiveBFourstolenkeybroker\ of the 100 per run. In E5, B5's reviewer released \VSZeroFiveBFiverecoveredn\ held observations per run after a median of \VSZeroFiveBFiverecpfifty~s from production, while B5s, without recovery, missed \VSZeroFiveBFiveSlegitmissing\ observations per run (\sys\ and B5: \VSZeroFiveBFivelegitmissing); after the benign sequence-reuse fault (E8), B5s lost \VSZeroEightBFiveSlegitmissing\ (\sys\ and B5: \VSZeroEightPBTIlegitmissing; P2 \VPTwoHolds). \subsection{Attacker-Model Sensitivity}\label{sec:res-attacker}

The impersonation attacker of the registered mixed workload is not fully consistent: transport duplicates of the genuine messages it suppresses reach the verifier, and a retransmitted or reordered forgery can contradict the attacker's own chain, so every pipeline received contradictions that a consistent attacker need not expose. An exploratory paired campaign repeated the mixed workload of the review-schedule evaluation on seeds 21--40 with an impersonator of the same capability (victims, window, keys, payload change) that re-sends one cached forgery per identity, chains predecessor links by sequence number and drops transport duplicates in the impersonated range (Table~\Sref{tab:S-v9-e04}). It raised the forged observations retained at the end of the run from \YEfourRegPBTIOOne\ to \YEfourConPBTIOOne\ per run for \sys\ and from \YEfourRegBhistOOne\ to \YEfourConBhistOOne\ for \Bhist, and the exposure of \sys\ from \YEfourRegPBTINLX\ to \YEfourConPBTINLX\ record-seconds. The ordering of the strategies persisted, but its size changed: the difference \Bhist\ minus \sys\ was \YEfourConBhistvsPBTINLX\ record-seconds of exposure per run (95\% CI [\YEfourConBhistvsPBTINLXLo, \YEfourConBhistvsPBTINLXHi]; \YEfourConBhistvsPBTINLXPct\% of the exposure of \sys, against \YEfourRegBhistvsPBTINLXPct\% under the registered attacker) and \YEfourConBhistvsPBTIW~s of withholding per observation, whereas with matched event-driven review B5 minus PBTI-R was \YEfourConBevvsRevNLX\ record-seconds of exposure and \YEfourConBevvsRevW~s of withholding (classes under the registered margins, used descriptively: \YEfourConBevvsRevNLXClass\ for exposure and \YEfourConBevvsRevWClass\ for withholding). The corrected and the historical B5 retained equally many forgeries in every seed and differed in these cell means by at most \YEfourHistCorrMaxOTwo\ lost observations, \YEfourHistCorrMaxNLX\ record-seconds of exposure and \YEfourHistCorrMaxW~s of withholding per run. Six deterministic control traces on the real verifiers (Table~\Sref{tab:S-v9-controls}) isolate the failure modes: every pipeline contests a forged version once a contradicting statement is admitted, and keeps it authoritative to the end when the successors are suppressed, when the attacker continues the feed consistently, when it writes identities that the device does not use, or when it opens a new epoch, after which the device's own statements are quarantined as stale until a reviewer, where present, releases them. Containment of either granularity therefore depends on contradicting evidence reaching the verifier, and the attack generator is a material source of uncertainty for retention and exposure magnitudes, which these runs do not settle for other workloads.

\subsection{Reviewed Release under Revoked Credentials}\label{sec:res-release}

The lifecycle analysis (Table~\Sref{tab:S-a5}) applies each release-evidence policy to the same hand-specified timelines on the real verifier, with keys compromised at $t_c$; \sys\ and B5 gave \WAfivePBTIBfiveSame\ results. The discretionary condition released a legitimate buffered record but also a backdated forgery produced with the compromised keys after $t_c$: a corroborating predecessor link satisfies the automated reviewer and a self-asserted time stamp the waiver, so the forgery became authoritative and, committed after the revocation had been processed, escaped taint. The witness policy refused the forgeries and released the legitimate record only when the witness had stamped its exact digest before the earliest plausible compromise time, accounting for the assumed witness-clock uncertainty; a witness outage withheld the record. Of the tested widths of the uncertainty interval around $t_c$, the record was recovered at \YEtenRecovered~s and withheld at \YEtenWithheld~s; these cases describe the specified record and witness timing and do not establish a general threshold. Under every policy and for every pipeline, a forgery contradicted by an admitted successor was rejected and a revoked reviewer could not decide; a decision bound to a superseded evidence snapshot was refused (at \YEnineRefusedAt~s, after new evidence had been admitted) and the case returned to quarantine, and the observation was recovered only through a later authorized decision on the current snapshot (at \YEnineFreshAt~s). The witness's key and clock are trusted by assumption, and the matrix is deterministic, not a campaign evaluation.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analyses, Replay, Audit and Ablation}\label{sec:res-sensitivity}

In the stressor sweep (Figure~\Sref{fig:S-sweep}), loss, duplicates, reordering, long delay and outages of up to 24~s changed neither outcome for any pipeline; B4 retained non-legitimate observations under equivocation (\VSwequivocationBFourOneTwenty\ per run at 20\%) and, lacking taint, after late compromise discovery, whereas \sys\ and B5 retained none and lost more legitimate observations instead, and B5 differed significantly from \sys\ in no sweep cell (exploratory), at the price of holds. The replay tests real sensor dynamics and timing, not attacks observed in a plant. The audit detected all seven insider tampering classes, including a rewrite re-signed with the evidence key, caught through the witness copies and decision replay (Table~\Sref{tab:S-audit}), and removing any one of the origin signature, the predecessor link, conflict detection, the horizon and epoch checks or revocation and taint significantly re-admitted non-legitimate observations on the mixed workload (\RAblnoprevlinknonlegitauth\ to \RAblnorevocationnonlegitauth\ per run versus \RAblfullnonlegitauth; Table~\Sref{tab:S-ablation}).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{RQ1: consequences of the containment granularity.} With the relay statement, path-bound admission and taint in place, the granularity of conflict containment changed final outcomes little in the tested conditions: in the 15 main scenarios and the four review-schedule workloads the feed-level comparator retained exactly as many forged observations as \sys, and its final losses differed only where its reviewer recovered data that \sys\ without a reviewer keeps quarantined, a difference that PBTI-R removes. Because B5 can retain fewer forgeries than PBTI-R with the same reviewer but never more (Section~\ref{sec:pipelines}), equal final counts mean that every forgery in these workloads either escaped the holds or was released as hash-link-consistent. This equality is scoped: when equivocation coincides with suppressed messages feed holds retain fewer forgeries (Section~\ref{sec:res-selective}), and a consistent impersonator defeats both granularities (Section~\ref{sec:res-attacker}). In the tested conditions the granularity changes \emph{when} genuine data are usable and \emph{how long} forged data are authoritative: per-identity containment changes the class and authority of at most two identities per admitted contradictory statement when no lifecycle transition intervenes (Proposition~\ref{prop:locality}) and keeps the rest of a feed available without a reviewer, whereas feed-level containment withholds the feed from the fork onward, forged observations included. With slow review this costs availability and saves exposure; with the same event-driven reviewer both differences disappear within the registered margins. B5's measured costs belong to the evaluated schedules and resume rule, not to feed-level designs in general; correcting the comparator's defects changed no registered class or claim gate (Section~\ref{sec:impl}).

\textbf{RQ2: conditions under which each is preferable, and common failures.} Table~\ref{tab:decision} lists them. Per-identity containment suits deployments in which contradictory evidence normally arrives and review is slow, unavailable or costly (E5: \XPrimEfiveBFivehistDEC\ automated decisions per run for \Bhist, \XPrimEfiveBFiveeventDEC\ for \Bev, none for \sys); feed-level containment suits those in which equivocation can coincide with suppression of the genuine version and its successors, because a forged version that no contradiction reaches stays authoritative under Rule L but held under a feed hold; selective holds sit between the two but failed their registered grid criterion. All evaluated rules fail when no contradiction reaches the verifier, as with full impersonation with both keys, forgery by the attesting gateway, statements under identities the device does not use, or a forged epoch advance, which also makes later genuine observations stale (Section~\ref{sec:res-attacker}); only a later revocation with a compromise time, or evidence from outside the signers' keys, contains these; a far-ahead identity can set its asset's current value under the (epoch, sequence) ordering (inferred, not tested). Mandatory predecessor links after the first sequence number, bounds on epoch advances and sequence jumps, and current values from chained statements only are untested hardening steps; reviewed release under a revoked key must not rely on a self-asserted time stamp.

\textbf{RQ3: costs.} Object-level binding cost \RTpRatioBTwo$\times$ the in-process throughput of B2, \RWireRatioBTwo$\times$ larger messages and \RDbRatioBTwo$\times$ the persisted bytes on the evaluated host; signature verification dominates the processing cost, and batching with parallel verification raised throughput at most \VBtPBTIbestgain-fold on two vCPUs. Strict identity semantics also turn benign sequence faults into loss, conservative taint discards legitimate data in the compromise window, and a strict horizon turns long outages into review work.

\textbf{Why the negative findings matter.} That a feed-level design built from published fork responses, with a reviewer of our design, matches the final outcomes of the per-identity rule in most tested workloads is expected in direction and was measured in size; once origin and relay evidence, path-bound admission and taint are in place, the remaining decision in these conditions is how long and how widely to withhold data after a fork (every principal finding and its evidence level: Table~\Sref{tab:S-findings}).

\textbf{Relation to prior work.} To the fork responses of signed-log systems and the convergence results of Section~\ref{sec:related}, this study adds an experimental account of what the response granularity does to individual industrial observations over time; it does not show that a feed-level response is inferior and does not evaluate branch-preserving designs such as GDP's~\cite{mor2019gdp}.

\textbf{Further limitations.} Accepted provenance does not establish physical measurement truth, and no evaluated rule detects forgery by an attesting gateway. The revocation scenario's attacker does not exploit the discretionary release condition that the lifecycle analysis shows to be exploitable. The relay binding's protection against a stolen device key assumes a device-to-gateway path that the attacker cannot write (otherwise an honest gateway relays the forged statement, as the symbolic model's variant shows), and the design trusts the credential and policy infrastructure, the verifier clock, durable epoch handling and a witness outside the verifier's control. Quarantine is receipt-specific, so a re-submission is evaluated afresh, and logging every rejected receipt is a storage denial-of-service concern; safety and actuation paths, confidentiality, physical process manipulation and flooding are outside the scope. \section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

Across the main scenarios and the matched-review workloads, containment granularity primarily changed the timing of genuine-data availability and of forged-data exposure, while final outcomes were identical or equivalent within the specified margins when review capability was matched. The feed-level comparator matched \sys's final forged admissions in all \VPOneIdentical\ main scenarios and its final losses in \VOTwoBFiveIdentical; its recovery advantage in the remaining scenario was removed when \sys\ received the same reviewer. With one-shot 60~s review, feed-level containment withheld genuine data longer and reduced exposure in the mixed workload; with matched event-driven review, the primary outcomes were identical or equivalent within the registered margins in the four review-schedule workloads. These hold for the corrected comparator, whose correction changed no class, gate or prediction.

These findings do not extend final-outcome equality to every attack condition. Under equivocation combined with suppression, feed-level holds retained fewer forgeries, and selective holds removed the retained forgeries in the amendment-informed factorial cells at the cost of additional genuine-data loss; the selective-hold grid did not meet its registered claim gates. A consistent impersonator, which leaks no contradiction, raised the forgeries retained at the end of the run from \YEfourRegPBTIOOne\ to \YEfourConPBTIOOne\ per run for \sys\ and to similar values for every other pipeline in an exploratory campaign. Rule L bounds direct identity-state changes to at most $2k$ for $k$ admitted statements only under the exclusions of Proposition~\ref{prop:locality}; it neither guarantees that contradictory evidence arrives nor detects a consistent impersonator holding the relevant keys. Reviewed release under revoked credentials requires evidence that compromised signers cannot create by backdating a statement.

The contribution is a conditional empirical account of containment scope, review timing, exposure and availability, not a new cryptographic primitive, a proof of physical measurement truth or evidence of the general superiority of either strategy. The immediate priority is an external, independent reproduction; separate-host and constrained-device measurements, a campaign-level evaluation of witness-based recovery, branch-preserving response policies, application-grounded deadlines and review costs, and plant data with recorded incidents remain further work.
