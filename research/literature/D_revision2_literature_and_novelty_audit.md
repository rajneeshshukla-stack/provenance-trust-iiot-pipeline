# Literature audit: novelty of Provenance-Bound Trust-Aware Ingestion (PBTI)

Prepared 3 November 2026 by an independent audit agent. Archived verbatim; its suggested wordings were not all adopted (in particular, no 'first' claim was adopted in the manuscript). This is a skeptical search for overlapping work. Its purpose is to find the strongest prior art, not to confirm novelty.

Baseline (not redone): the 15 closest works and 66 references already recorded in `research/CLOSEST_PRIOR_WORK.md`, `research/literature/*.md` and `paper/references.bib`. Everything below is **new** relative to that baseline, apart from cross-references.

**Access codes** (used in every matrix row)

- **SPEC**: official specification text; the relevant clauses were located and read.
- **ABS**: abstract only (sometimes also the first page).
- **META**: metadata only.
- **SEC**: content verified only through a secondary source.

**Wording rules**
- "(doc)" marks a limitation stated in the source itself.
- "(inf)" marks my inference. It is not stated by the source.
- Absence statements mean "not found in the examined text".

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

No source found combines all five PBTI elements for IIoT telemetry through a broker. Each element, however, has close and in several cases **standardized** prior art that the manuscript does not cite:

- **SCITT (RFC 9943, June 2026)** standardizes the core of PBTI's cloud verifier. Issuers' *signed statements* are admitted under a registration policy. The policy is versioned on the log and applied as of registration time. Admitted statements go into an RFC 9162 Merkle log with receipts, and auditors can *reproduce the registration checks*.
- **Secure Scuttlebutt (ICN 2019)** already uses PBTI's observation-statement structure: author key, sequence, hash of the previous message, claimed timestamp, content, and Ed25519 over canonical JSON. It also detects forks.
- **PeerReview (SOSP 2007)** and **Global Data Plane DataCapsules (ICDCS 2019)** turn equivocation in signed hash chains into evidence. DataCapsules name "holes" and "branches", which correspond to PBTI's LOST and CONTESTED.
- The **IETF ACE CoAP pub-sub profile (WG Last Call, 2026)** gives per-object publisher countersignatures (RFC 9338) end to end through an untrusted broker, with per-publisher sequence numbers and replay windows.
- **RFC 5280's invalidityDate** and **C2PA 2.3's revocation-versus-timestamp rule** already encode "compromise time" semantics.
- **Taser, Retro and Warp** (and Ammann et al. 2002) implement retroactive, dependency-driven "taint" and repair of derived state.

PBTI therefore reads as a **useful integration**: a SCITT-like transparency service specialized to high-rate IIoT telemetry. It adds one **narrow, meaningful improvement**: Rule L's order-independent, per-slot conflict semantics that use successor-implied digests. It also contributes a joint attack-and-fault evaluation.

Two of the manuscript's "genuinely new" claims need narrowing: (ii) workload identity bound into data provenance, and (iv) taint after retroactive revocation. Its B4 "strongest combination" baseline omits published techniques that would likely close most of B4's remaining gap to PBTI (Section (b)).

**C\*** is a combination a competent engineer could assemble only from the sources above plus those already cited:

**Outcome mapping** against the manuscript's own experiments. B4 is the paper's "strongest combination". It matched PBTI in 11 of 14 non-normal scenarios: E2, E3, E4, E6, E7, E9, E10, E11/12, E11b, E13, E15.

**What C\* would *not* reproduce without new design work**

1. A *single, explicitly specified* per-identity state model whose outcome depends only on the admitted evidence set, combining admitted versions with successor-implied digests. It must guarantee identical outcomes for every delivery order (Rule L, invariant I9) and keep unaffected slots authoritative. The components above use mutually incompatible conflict rules:
   - first-writer-wins in KIP-98, CloudEvents and the ACE replay window;
   - whole-feed invalidation in SSB;
   - strictly sequential validation in C2PA live video;
   - interpretation left to readers in GDP.
2. Origin-versus-relay *precision* of taint: do not taint device-signed data that a compromised workload merely relayed. It follows from the signature semantics but is specified nowhere I found.
3. Epoch namespaces for gateway-attested legacy devices interacting with that slot model.
4. The joint, paired-seed evaluation of attacks and delivery faults, which is independent of the mechanism.

All four are **design and evaluation contributions on top of existing techniques**. None is a new primitive.

**Implication for the manuscript's B4.** B4 is described as "the strongest combination of existing techniques we could assemble without the mechanisms specific to PBTI". However:
- the relay statement has a standard counterpart (RFC 9338 countersignatures, already used through a broker in the ACE profile);
- the predecessor-link contest has published counterparts (SSB, PeerReview, GDP);
- taint has published counterparts (Taser, Retro, Warp, Ammann et al.; invalidityDate).

Excluding them makes B4 weaker than the literature allows, and a reviewer can call it a straw man. Either:
- add a **B5 = B4 + countersigned relay + fork-evidence contest + revocation-triggered dependency repair**, and report how much of PBTI's advantage remains (expected: mainly order-independence and the E8 trade-off); or
- keep B4 but state explicitly that each excluded mechanism has published analogues, and claim the contribution as their specific integration and semantics.

## (c) Strongest evidence against PBTI's novelty (ranked)

1. **SCITT, RFC 9943 + RFC 9942 (IETF Standards Track, June 2026).** It is an architecture for admitting *signed statements* under a registration policy that is versioned on the log and applied as of registration time. Admitted statements go into an RFC 9162 Merkle log with receipts. Auditors must be able to reproduce every registration decision, and issuer equivocation is detectable. This pre-empts the architectural core of elements 3 and 5, including the "statement" vocabulary and the decision-replay audit. Not cited in the manuscript.
2. **Secure Scuttlebutt (ICN 2019).** It already uses PBTI's observation-statement structure (author key, sequence +1, previous-message hash, claimed timestamp, content, Ed25519 over canonical JSON) and detects forks. Element 1 is essentially SSB plus boot epoch and asset/schema fields.
3. **PeerReview (SOSP 2007), GDP DataCapsules (ICDCS 2019), Depot (TOCS 2011), Ethereum slashing.**
   - Equivocation and gap-fill turn into evidence through signed hash-chain commitments.
   - GDP names "holes" (≈ LOST) and "branches" (≈ CONTESTED) for IoT/edge data.
   - PeerReview's auditors replay logs against a reference implementation (≈ A-DET).
   - Rule L's *mechanisms* are classical; only its per-slot, order-independent *formulation* is new.
4. **IETF ACE CoAP pub-sub profile (WGLC 2026), RFC 9338, OSCORE/Group OSCORE.** Standards-track per-object publisher signatures through an untrusted broker, with per-publisher sequence numbers, replay windows and KDC-based authorization. This is the closest analogue of "identity crosses an untrusted broker" (CoAP rather than MQTT).
5. **RFC 5280 invalidityDate and C2PA 2.3 §15.9.2.**
   - "Revocation with a compromise time" is X.509's invalidityDate.
   - "Valid if signed (registered) before the compromise" is C2PA's and long-term-validation practice.
   - C2PA ingredients carry validation status through derivations.
6. **Taser (which literally reverts "tainted" operations), Retro, Warp, Ammann et al. 2002, ReComp.** Retroactive, dependency-graph-driven invalidation and recomputation of derived state after late discovery of compromise. This narrows gap (iv) in `B_closest_prior_work_records.md` to its IIoT, revocation-triggered instance.
7. **in-toto Statement v1 + Witness SPIFFE signer; WIMSE; NIST SP 800-207A.** Statements signed by a workload identity and bound to an artifact digest are established practice in software supply chains. This narrows gap (ii) ("no peer-reviewed work binding a SPIFFE-like workload identity into IoT data provenance") to the IoT-telemetry domain.
8. **Sparkplug 3.0 / ISO/IEC 20237:2023.**
   - The industrial MQTT standard already defines Edge-Node-scoped `seq`, `bdSeq`, a reorder timeout and rebirth resynchronization.
   - It has no payload security, which *supports* PBTI's motivation.
   - It must still be cited, because PBTI's event identity and gateway namespace closely mirror it.
9. **C2PA 2.3 live video (Dec 2025).** Signed per-segment manifests with sequence number and previous-manifest continuity.
10. **The manuscript's own B4 result.** It matches PBTI in 11 of 14 non-normal scenarios. A literature-faithful C\* would plausibly close most of the remaining three (E5, E14, E11b), leaving mainly the order-independence property and its E8 cost.

**What survives** (within what these searches surfaced):
- no single source integrates all five elements for IIoT telemetry through an MQTT broker;
- no source specifies Rule L's slot state as a function of admitted versions plus successor-implied digests, with explicit order-independence and a non-authoritative retry/replay label;
- no source specifies origin-key-precise taint of derived window aggregates triggered by revocation with a compromise time;
- no study jointly evaluates attacks and delivery faults in a reproducible IIoT pipeline.
